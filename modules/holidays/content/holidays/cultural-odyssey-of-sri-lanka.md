## What to prepare before travelling

Comfortable walking shoes are important because heritage sites often involve stone steps, uneven ground and exposed areas. Carry sun protection, water and modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees for religious sites; easy-to-remove footwear is useful where shoes must be left outside.

Keep travel documents and insurance available offline. If mobility is limited, flag it before booking so climbing-intensive or long walking sections can be reviewed rather than assumed suitable.

## Responsible travel on this route

Cultural sites should be visited as active religious and community places, not only as photo locations. Dress requirements, restricted areas and photography rules should be followed even when they affect the planned sequence. Local guides can add context and reduce the temptation to rush through several monuments without understanding them.

Where village or community experiences are part of the route, the operator should be able to explain what visitors are participating in and how the local hosts benefit. Original craft, food and guide services are preferable to staged interactions designed only for quick photographs.
