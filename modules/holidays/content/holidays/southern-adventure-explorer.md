## What to prepare before travelling

Pack around the active days: broken-in walking shoes, breathable clothing, rain protection, sun protection and a small day pack are more useful than formal travel gear. Any specialist activity should come with an operator-specific equipment list before departure. Travellers should disclose relevant mobility or fitness limitations early so the route can be adapted rather than discovered to be unsuitable on the day.

Carry booking confirmations and emergency information offline. Hydration, recovery time and realistic transfer windows are part of the itinerary design, especially when active days sit next to long road sectors.

## Responsible travel on this route

Adventure activities should only run when the local operator considers conditions safe. Weather, water levels, trail access and equipment availability can change quickly, and cancelling or shortening an activity is preferable to forcing the published plan. Guides should follow site rules and leave-no-trace principles on trails and natural areas.

Community and village experiences should be paced as genuine visits rather than staged photo opportunities. Where local guides or small operators are involved, the tour should make clear what service they provide and how the visit contributes economically.
